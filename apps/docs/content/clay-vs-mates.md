# Clay Studio, Clay, and other Mates

**Clay Studio** is the product: the application, workspace, and server where you manage projects, sessions, and collaborators. **Clay** is its lead Mate, your owner-level collaborator for supported workspace operations and routing project work. Clay is a Mate's name, not a shortened name for the product.

Other **Mates** are persistent collaborators with their own identities, instructions, Knowledge, Memory, and conversations. They can serve roles such as researcher or reviewer without receiving all of Clay's owner-scoped controls.

## Choose whom to ask

Use Clay for a workspace request such as finding relevant work, inspecting supported records, or assigning a bounded project task through the reviewed Driver path.

Use another Mate for a continuing role: a research partner, technical reviewer, or planning collaborator. Give it a useful remit and maintain its Knowledge rather than recreating the role in every prompt.

Use a project session for work grounded in a repository. A project Driver owns execution and Worker review. A Mate does not automatically become a project Driver by being asked to act like one.

## Context and privacy

Conversations with Clay or another Mate are private to their owner. Ordinary project administrator access does not grant their transcript or Knowledge.

A contextual floating conversation can discuss the workspace you are viewing. Check its context indication before asking it to act. Opening the native conversation gives more room while retaining the exact conversation; it should not be confused with starting a new one.

Private context should be summarized deliberately before it enters a shared project record.

## Canonical controls and limits

Canonical Clay uses typed, authorized product controls for supported actions. A review identifies the exact mutation and target. Read it before approving. Copied transcript text or another agent's message is not approval.

Server Settings and User Settings are excluded from canonical control. Change them yourself in their settings surfaces. Worker creation/execution belongs to project Drivers; Clay routes authorized assignments and follow-ups through those reviewed paths.

Not every visible contextual action is a canonical tool. For example, the native background-task viewer has human controls beyond the generic task count Clay can observe.

## Observe outcomes

A proposal, queued assignment, started session, saved record, and visibly rendered navigation are different outcomes. If Clay reports partial failure or uncertainty, inspect the relevant destination before retrying.
