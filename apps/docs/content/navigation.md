# Navigate Clay Studio

On desktop, the full-width top bar sits above the workspace. Beneath it, the project and Mate **rail** and the conversation **sidebar** have separate surfaces, with a compact gap between them and another gap before the working area. The rail holds project and Mate icons in a compact Dock face and stays visible when you collapse the sidebar. A project's **sidebar** lists its conversations and saved Views. The main area shows the selected conversation; the composer sends to that conversation. The **tool palette** opens panels such as Files or Terminal beside your work.

The desktop workspace uses **Clay Gray** by default. In **Settings → Personal → Appearance → Workspace background**, choose a circular color swatch or select **Clay Embossed**, **Classic Mac Dither**, **Clay Memphis**, **Clay Clouds**, **Clay Mates**, **Clay Meadow**, **Clay Silicon**, **Clay Aqua**, **Clay Light**, **Clay Galaxy**, or **Clay Cove** under **Pictures**. The eight historical swatches keep the same colors across appearances; Clay Gray and custom color pairs follow your light or dark appearance. Clay Embossed keeps its periwinkle repeating artwork in both appearances; Classic Mac Dither keeps a black-and-white checkerboard with two-pixel squares. Clay Memphis, Clay Clouds, Clay Mates, Clay Meadow, Clay Silicon, Clay Aqua, Clay Light, Clay Galaxy, and Clay Cove fill the window with centered artwork that stays the same in both appearances. Existing saved colors stay selected, and your content palette stays separate. Use **+** beside the color strip to add your own light and dark pair; [workspace background settings](/docs/account-devices-security#choose-the-workspace-background) explains the picker and saving. The Dock face and sidebar use a softly blurred surface. The workspace color or picture shows faintly through them. The top menu bar and pane headers stay fully opaque. Conversation reading surfaces and the Worker chip row have opaque backgrounds. Conversation text and tool content stay sharp. If your browser requests reduced transparency or cannot display backdrop blur, the surfaces use a solid background instead.

## Choose where to work

Entering a project returns to your last visited session while it is running or within 24 hours of work stopping. Otherwise, Clay Studio opens your most recently visited running session, or shows the centered session picker. Your visit order is saved for your account across devices. Selecting a particular conversation always opens that conversation. [Sessions](/docs/sessions) explains the return rules.

Select a project in the rail, expand a session folder or **Unfiled**, then select a conversation. A saved **View** opens several panes together. To add a project or Mate, select the rail's **+** button, labeled **Add Mate or project**.

Click a Mate icon to open its floating conversation; click again to enter its full workspace. [Projects and Mates](/docs/clay-vs-mates) explains when to use each.

On smaller screens, open the navigation menu to choose a destination, then return to the conversation.

At short desktop window heights, scroll the rail to reach the remaining projects, Mates, and **+** button; your account controls stay at the bottom.

On desktop, choose **Collapse sidebar** to keep only the rail visible. Choose **Open sidebar** in the rail, just below the Clay entry, to restore the session list and tool palette.

## Organize project conversations

Use a session's context menu for **Rename**, **Move to folder**, and **Add to Favorites**. Right-click its row to open the menu. A favorite stays in its original folder and working directory.

Choose **New folder** in the folder toolbar, enter a name, and confirm. To rename or delete a custom folder, right-click its header, long-press on touch, or focus it and press **Shift+F10**. When deleting, read the choice of what happens to its sessions.

Folders organize conversations; they do not move repository files. For example, a “Release” folder can hold planning and verification sessions that still use the same project directory.

## Sort and find work

**View options** controls sorting within folders: **Last activity**, **Date created**, **Title**, or **Manual order**. Optional date headings subdivide those folders. These display options are separate from a saved **View**, which is a pane arrangement.

Press **Command+K** on macOS or **Ctrl+K** elsewhere to open the command palette. Type a destination or command, choose a result with arrow keys, and press Enter. Escape closes it. Use [session search](/docs/session-history-search) for conversation content.

If a conversation seems missing, clear search, expand folders, and check the selected project and account before creating another one.

## Arrange the workspace

Use [Split Views](/docs/split-views) to keep several conversations visible. Tools open over the workspace; **Dock** reserves room beside the conversations. [Workbench layout](/docs/workbench-layout) explains Dock, Float, Expand, and Close.

Appearance settings live in **Settings → Personal → Appearance**. Choose your theme, conversation layout, and typography. The layout previews show the difference between separate centered bubbles and full-width conversations with avatars. Terminal has its own font settings and retains a dark content area.
